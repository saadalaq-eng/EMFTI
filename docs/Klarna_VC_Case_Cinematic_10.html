Part II · Venture capital perspective

What they saw — and what they missed

The thesis was not crazy
01
Network effects

Consumer scale attracted merchants; merchant acceptance reinforced consumer usage — harder to displace with every new merchant.

02
Platform optionality

Win checkout, then expand into cards, banking, financing, memberships and ads — a larger share of the consumer's financial life.

+ Category shift+ Distribution in checkout+ Founder-led record+ Scarcity premium
The missed risk wasn't BNPL default — it was regime change
↯
Multiple risk

Private price hinged on public fintech multiples.

%
Funding & rate risk

Credit economics hostage to the rate regime.

◔
Credit · regulation · competition

Provisioning, BNPL rules, Affirm / PayPal / banks.

Cost of capital

The cost of capital moved faster than the growth narrative could adjust. A company can execute well and still reset when the discount rate moves.

Who created value

Part III · Three lessons

What Klarna teaches a VC

01
Great company ≠ great entry price

Returns are company quality × valuation. Priced for perfection, even strong execution can't protect the investor.

02
Stress-test the capital regime

Model higher funding costs, lower public multiples, slower exits. Cheap capital is never the base case forever.

03
Governance must match risk

Independent directors with credit and regulatory depth, profitability milestones, and exit planning before price detaches from public comps.

Our investment committee verdict
Invest— but not at the 2021 peak

Price linked to public comparables · path to profitable growth · explicit funding-cost stress tests · stronger governance protections.

Discipline matters most when everyone else is optimistic.
